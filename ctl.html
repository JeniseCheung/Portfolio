Collegiate Tetris League (CTL)

is a tournament series aimed towards post-secondary school students in Canada and the United States. CTL is a collaborative event between UBCEA (University of British Columbia’s eSports’ Association) and various online modern Tetris tournament organizers.

The goal of the Collegiate Tetris League is to encourage North American post-secondary school students to engage more with their local Tetris communities, as well as welcome new players into the modern Tetris scene.

PLANNING & SIGNUPS

Communication and Organization

Meeting Notes

Discord To Do List

We communicated through Discord and scheduled meetings using When2Meet. For each session, I organized meeting notes in Google Docs using tabs and headers so they were easy to navigate. I also created to-do tables to record what each person was in charge of.

We pivoted to using Discord as our main way of delegating and doing tasks, streamlining communication to keep everything more organized. We used forums to organize our to-do list and threads for a more structured and easier to follow workflow.

PLANNING & SIGNUPS

Format Planning and Testing

Proposed Changes & Feedback

We established format changes, the range of how many weeks the tournament would take, and types of prizes.

We introduced the potential changes to the team captains and had teams participate in scrimmages to test the format changes. After, I interviewed players, gathered feedback, and created a summary covering the new format, prizes, scheduling, and other concerns and suggestions. In the next meeting, we made changes accordingly.

FUNDING

Setting Up & Managing Prize
Pool Incentives

Announcement for Volunteers

CTL is volunteer-based and didn't have as much funding or sponsors as last year. Therefore, we proposed a registration fee. After I collected more feedback from teams and players, we chose to bring back prize pool incentives instead as a fee would make the event less appealing. I also created a volunteer commentators form for streams and feedback for prize pool incentives.

DURING THE EVENT

Coordinating Matches

I sent a reminder in the team captains channel asking them to create scheduling polls every Monday. I would provide a poll template that used dynamic date-time formatting so users could view times in their own timezone.

To organize matches, I created threads for each match so teams could communicate and agree on a timeslot. I monitored the threads to make sure teams were sending their polls, and once a match time was decided, teams would tag the organizers to record it in the spreadsheet. If this wasn’t done, I followed up by tagging the team captains and helping coordinate scheduling to finalize matches.

Reflection

What I've learned

I gained a deeper understanding of competitive Tetris, including game analysis, evaluating teams and individual stats, drafting strategies, and match analysis. Additionally, I learned more about how tournaments are run.

Challenges

I want to be more prepared for next time and improve the planning process. I wasn't always sure what was needed and what to prioritize. For example, some things like the registration poster and rules video were made a bit late close to the registration deadline, and I created some graphics late at night because they were requested on short notice. Scheduling was also challenging due to limited organizer availability, and inconsistent team responsiveness.

Future Considerations

To improve this, I will look into the feedback form responses, discuss key points with the team, and share a document outlining things to consider for the next iteration of the tournament. This will include a checklist of required materials, such as forms and graphics, to ensure everything is prepared on time.

Update

Before our retrospective meeting, I created a document with a table addressing the key topics we wanted to discuss. I referred to the feedback form to support my points and recommendations.

I also created a table outlining a rough estimate of the CTL preparation timeline, breaking down what we should do before, during, at the end of, and after the event. In addition, I designed a visual timeline in Figma as a supporting reference to make the process easier to understand, including required actions, posters, and announcements, along with notes on what would be needed for each stage.
